03-L-001 y=95.0-106.0 3.1.1 Velocity-Threshold Identification (I-VT)
03-L-002 y=108.0-117.0 Velocity-threshold fixation identification (I-VT) is the
03-L-003 y=119.0-128.0 simplest of the identification methods to understand and
03-L-004 y=129.9-138.9 implement. I-VT is a velocity-based method that separates
03-L-005 y=140.9-149.9 fixation and saccade points based on their point-to-point
03-L-006 y=151.9-160.9 velocities [e.g., 5, 18]. The velocity profiles of saccadic eye
03-L-007 y=162.9-171.9 movements show essentially two distributions of velocities:
03-L-008 y=173.9-182.9 low velocities for fixations (i.e., <100 deg/sec), and high
03-L-009 y=184.9-193.9 velocities (i.e., >300 deg/sec) for saccades. This aspect of
03-L-010 y=195.9-204.9 saccadic eye movements makes velocity-based discrimination
03-L-011 y=206.9-215.9 fairly straightforward and robust. While it is possible to create
03-L-012 y=217.9-226.9 a locally adaptive velocity-based fixation identification
03-L-013 y=228.9-237.9 algorithm, velocity profiles have strong physical and
03-L-014 y=239.9-248.9 physiological underpinnings, and thus static criteria are
03-L-015 y=250.9-259.9 usually sufficient here.
03-L-016 y=265.9-274.9 I-VT begins by calculating point-to-point velocities for each
03-L-017 y=276.9-285.9 point in the protocol. Each velocity is computed as the
03-L-018 y=287.9-296.9 distance between the current point and the next (or previous)
03-L-019 y=298.9-307.9 point. I-VT then classifies each point as a fixation or saccade
03-L-020 y=309.9-318.9 point based on a simple velocity threshold: if the point’s
03-L-021 y=320.9-329.9 velocity is below threshold, it becomes a fixation point,
03-L-022 y=331.9-340.9 otherwise it becomes a saccade point. The process then
03-L-023 y=342.9-351.9 collapses consecutive fixation points into fixation groups and
03-L-024 y=353.9-362.9 discards saccade points. Finally, I-VT translates each fixation
03-L-025 y=364.9-373.9 group to a representation <x,y,t,d> using the centroid (i.e.,
03-L-026 y=375.9-384.9 center of mass) of the points as x and y, the time of the first
03-L-027 y=386.9-395.9 point as t, and the duration of the points as d. Pseudocode for
03-L-028 y=397.9-406.9 the I-VT method is shown in Table 2.

03-R-001 y=91.0-100.0 Hidden Markov model fixation identification (I-HMM) uses
03-R-002 y=102.0-111.0 probabilistic analysis to determine the most likely
03-R-003 y=113.0-122.0 identifications for a given protocol [15, 16]. Hidden Markov
03-R-004 y=124.0-132.9 models (HMMs) are probabilistic finite state machines that
03-R-005 y=134.9-143.9 have been employed extensively in the fields of speech and
03-R-006 y=145.9-154.9 handwriting recognition [see 13]. I-HMM uses a two-state
03-R-007 y=156.9-165.9 HMM in which the states represent the velocity distributions
03-R-008 y=167.9-176.9 for saccade and fixation points. This probabilistic
03-R-009 y=178.9-187.9 representation helps I-HMM perform more robust
03-R-010 y=189.9-198.9 identification than a fixed-threshold method such as I-VT.
03-R-011 y=204.9-213.9 The crux of I-HMM is the two-state HMM shown in Figure 1.
03-R-012 y=215.9-224.9 The HMM includes two sets of probabilities: observation and
03-R-013 y=226.9-235.9 transition probabilities. The observation probabilities for
03-R-014 y=237.9-246.9 each state (the small v distributions) represent the distribution
03-R-015 y=248.9-257.9 of expected velocities in that state. The first state represents
03-R-016 y=259.9-268.9 saccade points, and thus contains a distribution centered around
03-R-017 y=270.9-279.9 higher velocities; the second state represents fixation points,
03-R-018 y=281.9-290.9 and thus contains a distribution centered around lower
03-R-019 y=292.9-301.9 velocities. The transition probabilities for each state (the
03-R-020 y=303.9-312.9 arrows exiting the states) represent the likelihood of remaining
03-R-021 y=314.9-323.9 in the state or making a transition to another state. The
03-R-022 y=325.9-334.9 transition probabilities in Figure 1 show a large likelihood of
03-R-023 y=336.9-345.9 remaining in each state (.95) and a small likelihood or making
03-R-024 y=347.9-356.9 a transition (.05). Thus, the HMM provides a probabilistic
03-R-025 y=358.9-367.9 representation of the observations (i.e., velocities) generated
03-R-026 y=369.9-378.9 during saccadic eye movements.

04-L-036 y=616.0-627.0 3.2.1 Dispersion-Threshold Identification (I-DT)
04-L-037 y=629.0-638.0 In contrast to the velocity-based identification of I-VT and I-
04-L-038 y=640.0-649.0 HMM, dispersion-threshold identification (I-DT) utilizes the
04-L-039 y=651.0-660.0 fact that fixation points, because of their low velocity, tend to
04-L-040 y=662.0-671.0 cluster closely together. I-DT identifies fixations as groups of
04-L-041 y=673.0-682.0 consecutive points within a particular dispersion, or maximum
04-L-042 y=684.0-693.0 separation [e.g., 19, 21]. Because fixations typically have a
04-L-043 y=695.0-704.0 duration of at least 100 ms, dispersion-based identification
04-L-044 y=706.0-715.0 techniques often incorporate a minimum duration threshold of
04-R-000 y=79.0-88.0 100-200 ms [21] to help alleviate equipment variability. The
04-R-001 y=90.0-99.0 following I-DT algorithm is based on Widdel’s [21] data
04-R-002 y=101.0-110.0 reduction algorithm.
04-R-003 y=116.0-125.0 The I-DT algorithm uses a moving window that spans
04-R-004 y=127.0-135.9 consecutive data points checking for potential fixations. The
04-R-005 y=137.9-146.9 moving window begins at the start of the protocol and initially
04-R-006 y=148.9-157.9 spans a minimum number of points, determined by the given
04-R-007 y=159.9-168.9 duration threshold and sampling frequency. I-DT then checks
04-R-008 y=170.9-179.9 the dispersion of the points in the window by summing the
04-R-009 y=181.9-190.9 differences between the points’ maximum and minimum x and y
04-R-010 y=192.9-201.9 values; in other words, dispersion D = [max(x) – min(x)] +
04-R-011 y=203.9-212.9 [max(y) – min(y)]. Note that alternative dispersion metrics
04-R-012 y=214.9-223.9 could be based upon spatial variance or area of samples. If the
04-R-013 y=225.9-234.9 dispersion is above the dispersion threshold, the window does
04-R-014 y=236.9-245.9 not represent a fixation, and the window moves one point to
04-R-015 y=247.9-256.9 the right. If the dispersion is below the dispersion threshold,
04-R-016 y=258.9-267.9 the window represents a fixation. In this case, the window is
04-R-017 y=269.9-278.9 expanded (to the right) until the window’s dispersion is above
04-R-018 y=280.9-289.9 threshold. The final window is registered as a fixation at the
04-R-019 y=291.9-300.9 centroid of the window points with the given onset time and
04-R-020 y=302.9-311.9 duration. This process continues with window moving to the
04-R-021 y=313.9-322.9 right until the end of the protocol is reached. Table 4 includes
04-R-022 y=324.9-333.9 pseudocode for the I-DT algorithm.
04-R-023 y=339.9-348.9 We can note that this characterization of fixations uses the

05-R-008 y=178.0-189.0 3.3.1 Area-of-Interest Identification (I-AOI)
05-R-009 y=190.9-199.9 The four previous identification methods can identify fixations
05-R-010 y=201.9-210.9 at any location in the visual field. In contrast, area-of-interest
05-R-011 y=212.9-221.9 fixation identification (I-AOI) identifies only fixations that
05-R-012 y=223.9-232.9 occur within specified target areas [e.g., 3]. The target areas are
05-R-013 y=234.9-243.9 rectangular regions of interest that represent units of
05-R-014 y=245.9-254.9 information in the visual field. These target areas, generally
05-R-015 y=256.9-265.9 used in later analyses like tracing, keep identified fixations
05-R-016 y=267.9-276.9 close to relevant targets. I-AOI also utilizes a duration
05-R-017 y=278.9-287.9 threshold to help distinguish fixations in target areas from
05-R-018 y=289.9-298.9 passing saccades in those areas.
05-R-019 y=304.9-313.9 I-AOI begins by associating data points with target areas: it
05-R-020 y=315.9-324.9 labels points within a target area as a fixation point for that
05-R-021 y=326.9-335.9 target, and labels points outside of all target areas as saccades.
05-R-022 y=337.9-346.9 I-AOI then collapses consecutive fixation points for the same
05-R-023 y=348.9-357.9 target into fixation groups, discarding saccade points. Finally,
05-R-024 y=359.9-368.9 it removes fixation groups that fall below a given duration
05-R-025 y=370.9-379.9 threshold and transforms each fixation group into a fixation
